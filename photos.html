MATERIA.

We keep giving our thoughts a shape. Strange how they sometimes end up saying something else.

I've never been particularly fond of going out or socializing face to face. Fortunately, there are people who find other ways to communicate with the world — through architecture, art, and the things they create.

There's something comforting about that. I find a quiet sense of reassurance in these forms of expression, and in the possibility of understanding someone without ever having to meet them.

MARSALA.

There is a certain elegance in colors that never demand attention. Warm yet restrained, Marsala possesses a quiet richness that feels especially at home in Giorgio Morandi's world, accentuating a subtle yet somehow unforgettable presence.

TABLEAU.

An exhibition is never quite complete without its visitors. Art, architecture, and the people passing through them become part of a composition of their own. Some may question the objectifying gaze directed at unsuspecting strangers. But who is to say where the exhibition ends and its audience begins?

HEPHAESTUS.

Every material has a character of its own, and every craft is a quiet negotiation with it. There is a certain elegance in knowing when to shape, when to yield, and when to let something unexpected take form.

TEMPORAL PARADOX.

Came across this piece at an exhibition of works by preschool children. Perhaps I've underestimated what someone so young is capable of. Still, the possibility that its creator had somehow traveled through time seems strangely convincing. I suppose some mysteries are better left unsolved.

AFTERLIFE.

Few could have imagined that a slaughterhouse built nearly a century ago would one day be celebrated for its symphony of light and shade. Time has a curious way of changing what we find beautiful, and what we choose to remember.

VANITAS.

Art does not always begin with artistic intent. Sometimes, it is simply a matter of how others choose to see it. Imagine spending everything you have saved on a modest home, only to watch its value fall by half five years later. There is a certain tragic absurdity in that arithmetic, one that few contemporary art exhibitions could ever hope to rival.

TERRA.

The world keeps changing the light, as if it hasn't quite decided what it wants to look like.

Light moves across the landscape, changing what comes forward and what slips away. The same place can hold a different photograph each time.

A few moments collected along the way: leaves against the sky, a distant horizon, a patch of colour that made me stop.

MECHANICA.

Everyone needs something to keep them grounded. Funny how mine usually have wheels.

Curves, joints, reflections. There is a rhythm to the things we build, even when they are standing still.

These photographs linger on the details of movement: a line drawn through metal, a wheel catching the light, the space around a machine at rest.
